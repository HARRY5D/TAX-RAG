# ITR Filing — Who Must File, Which Form to Use, FY 2025-26

## Who Must File an Income Tax Return?

Filing an ITR is mandatory if ANY of the following conditions apply:
1. Gross total income (before deductions) exceeds the basic exemption limit:
   - Old Regime: Rs.2,50,000
   - New Regime: Rs.3,00,000
   - Senior Citizens (60-79 years): Rs.3,00,000 (Old Regime)
   - Super Senior Citizens (80+ years): Rs.5,00,000 (Old Regime)
2. You have deposited more than Rs.1 crore in bank accounts in a year
3. You spent more than Rs.2 lakh on foreign travel in a year
4. You spent more than Rs.1 lakh on electricity in a year
5. You have foreign assets or foreign income
6. You are a company or firm (irrespective of profit or loss)
7. TDS or TCS has been deducted and you want to claim refund

Even if TDS is fully deducted by employer, you MUST still file ITR if:
- You have other income (FD interest, savings interest, rental income) that exceeds limits
- You want to claim a refund of excess TDS
- Your total gross income exceeds the basic exemption limit from all sources combined

## Fixed Deposit (FD) Interest — Taxability and TDS

### Tax Treatment
FD interest is taxable as "Income from Other Sources" (not salary).
It must be added to your total income and taxed at applicable slab rates.

### TDS on FD Interest — Section 194A
| Condition | TDS Rate | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Non-senior citizen | 10% | Interest > Rs.40,000 per year per bank |
| Senior citizen (60+) | 10% | Interest > Rs.50,000 per year per bank |
| If PAN not provided | 20% | Any amount |

Important: TDS is deducted by the bank, but you must still declare the FULL FD interest
in your ITR (not just the net amount after TDS). The TDS is a pre-payment of tax.

### Savings Account Interest — Section 80TTA and 80TTB
- Section 80TTA: Deduction of up to Rs.10,000 on savings account interest (NOT FD interest).
  Available ONLY under Old Regime. For non-senior citizens only.
- Section 80TTB: Senior citizens (60+) can claim up to Rs.50,000 on ALL interest income
  (savings + FD + post office), but only under Old Regime.
- Both 80TTA and 80TTB are Chapter VI-A deductions — NOT available under New Regime.

## Which ITR Form to Use?

### ITR-1 (Sahaj) — Simplest Form
**Who can use:**
- Resident individual (not HUF or company)
- Total income up to Rs.50 lakh
- Income sources: salary/pension + one house property + other sources (FD interest, savings interest, family pension)
- Agricultural income up to Rs.5,000

**Who CANNOT use ITR-1:**
- If you have capital gains (stock, mutual fund, property sale)
- If you have more than one house property
- If you are a director in a company
- If you have foreign assets or foreign income
- If total income exceeds Rs.50 lakh

### ITR-2 — For More Complex Income
**Who should use:**
- Income above Rs.50 lakh
- Capital gains from sale of property, shares, or mutual funds
- More than one house property
- Foreign assets or foreign income
- Director of a company or has unlisted shares
- Non-resident or not ordinarily resident

### ITR-3 — Business/Profession Income
- For individuals/HUFs with income from business or profession
- Freelancers, consultants, professionals (doctors, lawyers, CAs)
- Partners in a firm

### ITR-4 (Sugam) — Presumptive Taxation
- For freelancers/small businesses opting for Section 44ADA or 44AD
- Total income up to Rs.50 lakh
- Cannot have capital gains or foreign income

## Common Scenario: Salaried + FD Interest

If you are a salaried employee with TDS deducted by employer AND you have FD interest income:
1. You MUST file ITR (FD interest is income from other sources)
2. Use ITR-1 if total income < Rs.50 lakh and no capital gains
3. Report FD interest in "Income from Other Sources" schedule
4. Claim TDS credit from Form 26AS
5. Claim savings interest deduction under Section 80TTA (Old Regime only, max Rs.10,000)
6. If excess TDS was deducted, you will receive a refund
